## 8. Systemic Diagnosis

### 8.1 Friendship Over Contribution

Informal payments (Alisha → accessor.eth) without contracts create arbitrary cutoff. Insiders receive structured packages; watchdogs receive gratitude then abandonment.

### 8.2 Rational Ignorance Arbitrage

Complex treasury data exceeds median delegate audit capacity. accessor.eth performed unpaid CFO/CRO functions while compensated roles focused on process.

### 8.3 Disposable Contributor Mentality

High-velocity auditors treated as volunteers; external brand vendors (Lemma) preferred over internal competence.

### 8.4 Performative vs. Productive Governance

Forum engagement correlates with delegation success — incentivizes posting over building; accessor.eth bridged both but lacked electoral conversion.

---

## 9. Structural Reforms to Prevent Recurrence

1. **Mandatory RPGF window** for identified public goods with third-party triage
2. **Impact-based compensation** decoupled from steward electoral politics
3. **Formal contracts** for any recurring contributor payments >$1K/month
4. **COI registry** and recusal for budget self-dealing
5. **Red Team audit bounty** permanently funded (≥$50K/yr)
6. **Contributor Gateway Tier 3** — core stream path for proven auditors/architects
7. **IP attribution policy** for forum-origin drafts used in paid RFPs

## 10. Volume V Conclusions

accessor.eth is not a peripheral critic but a **load-bearing accountability node** whose exclusion correlates with the DAO's weakest control areas (PG variance, shadow recipients, fragmented reporting). The $213,900 RPGF figure is a **correction signal**, not merely back pay. Without contributor-economy reform, the DAO will lose adversarial diligence and retain relational funding — the exact combination Volume VI aims to dismantle.
